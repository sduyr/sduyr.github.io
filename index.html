Senjuti Dutta

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Center for the Dynamics of Social Complexity (DySoC) at the University of Tennessee, Knoxville. I work in human-computer interaction (HCI). My research asks how intelligent systems can better support human reasoning and real-world work, and how people make decisions with AI.

My PhD research investigated the gap between the promised flexibility of crowdwork and the rigid workflows enforced by current platforms. Through empirical studies across smartphones, tablets, smart speakers, and smartwatches, I examined how crowdworkers adapt their practices to different devices. At Google Research, I studied how human feedback can be collected and modeled to build higher-quality AI systems. Building on this work, I now design adaptive AI systems that align with people’s cognitive processes, goals, and contexts, and I study how teams reason and make decisions in high-stakes settings. I am currently studying how the way AI expresses uncertainty affects how people make decisions.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Tennessee, Knoxville in 2024. Before DySoC, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Colorado Boulder.

How teams reason and make decisions in high-stakes settings

2025 – now

Teams in high-stakes settings must make fast decisions with incomplete or contradictory information. However, we still know little about how a team notices that something is wrong, questions it, and decides what to do next.

We study this question through team communication and metacognition. Our first step maps which parts of annotating team communication large language models can take on, and where human judgment remains essential. This shows how AI can help researchers analyze team decision making at a scale that manual coding alone cannot reach, while keeping people in charge of the judgments that matter.

Keywords: Decision making, Metacognition, AI-enabled analysis, LLM-assisted annotation

Human feedback for higher-quality AI models

2022 – 2024

Generative AI systems learn from human judgments. However, people judge the same output differently, based on their background, experience, and goals. Averaging these judgments away hides the very signal that makes them useful.

We study how to capture this subjectivity instead. Our work models annotator disagreement in toxic comment identification across different groups of annotators, shows how motivational context shapes satisfaction with model-generated images, and untangles the challenges of collecting fine-grained feedback for text-to-image generation. We also contributed to a design space for intelligent writing assistants and to an evaluation framework for generative story-writing tools. Together, this work helps AI developers collect better human data and build models that serve people more reliably.

Keywords: Human data for AI, AI evaluation, Annotation, Text-to-image, Writing assistants

Flexible digital work across devices

2020 – 2025

Crowdwork promises flexibility: work from anywhere, at any time. However, most platforms still assume a worker sits at a desktop computer, which starkly contrasts with how and where people actually work.

We investigate this gap through empirical studies across smartphones, tablets, smart speakers, and smartwatches. We examine how crowdworkers adapt their practices to different devices, and which inter-related preferences and traits shape these choices. Our overall findings indicate that a one-size-fits-all design leaves many workers behind. We translate these findings into design implications for personalized and flexible work platforms, including AI tools that match tasks to a worker’s device and context. This work speaks to how the workforce adapts as AI changes where and how work gets done.

Keywords: Future of work, Workforce, Crowdsourcing, Mobile and wearable work, Personalization
